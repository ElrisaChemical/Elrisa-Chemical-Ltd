What we supply

The binder behind every brake pad and grinding wheel

Phenolic resin is what holds a friction material or a bonded abrasive together. Mixed with fibres, fillers and abrasives, then hot-pressed, it melts, wets every particle and cures into a rigid matrix that keeps its strength at braking and grinding temperatures.

Elrisa Chemicals supplies the ELRIBOND range: phenol-formaldehyde novolacs with and without hexamine, rubber, organic, inorganic and CNSL-modified grades, high-bond pre-reacted novolacs, solid resols, liquid CNSL resins and CNSL friction particles. Every grade has published typical properties, so your technical team can shortlist before asking for a sample.

Need a resin made from renewable cashew chemistry instead? See our phenol-free phenolic resin and CNSL for friction materials.

How to choose

Reading a phenolic resin data sheet

Four numbers decide most choices. Here is what each one means for your process.

Flow · mm

How far the resin travels

Plate flow at 125 °C. Short-flow grades (12–25 mm) stay where they are put, which suits high-pressure moulding and fine mixes. Long-flow grades (50–100 mm) wet out fibres and coarse fillers more fully.

Gel time · s

How fast it cures

Seconds to gel at 150 °C. Shorter gel times mean shorter press cycles and higher output. Fast-curing grades gel in 20–70 s; standard grades in 70–140 s.

Hexamine · %

How much curing agent

Hexamine crosslinks a novolac. Around 9 % gives full cure; lower levels release less ammonia and suit sensitive mixes. Hexamine-free grades let you add your own hardener.

Melting point · °C

How it handles and mixes

Higher melting points keep powder free-flowing in hot climates and storage; lower melting points start flowing sooner in the press.

FAQ

Phenolic resin questions

What is phenolic resin used for in brake pads?

Phenolic resin is the binder that holds a brake pad together. The fibres, fillers, abrasives and lubricants are mixed with a novolac powder resin and hot-pressed; the resin melts, flows around the other ingredients and cures into a rigid, heat-resistant matrix. It keeps its strength at the high temperatures reached during braking, which is why it is used in almost all brake pads, drum linings and clutch facings.

What is the difference between novolac and resol phenolic resins?

A novolac is made with excess phenol and needs a curing agent, usually hexamine, to harden. A resol is made with excess formaldehyde and cures on its own with heat. Novolac powders are the usual choice for friction materials and bonded abrasives; resols are used where a binder without added hexamine is wanted.

How do I choose the right phenolic resin grade?

Start with flow (how far the molten resin travels before it gels), then cure speed (gel time) and hexamine content. Then pick a modification for the job: NBR or CNSL for lower noise and more comfort, phosphorus or boron for high temperatures, boron or chromite for a higher friction coefficient, and fast-curing or high-bond grades for shorter press cycles. Our team will recommend a shortlist if you tell us your application.

Store it below 20 °C in the unopened original bags, in a dry place away from heat. Under these conditions most ELRIBOND powder grades keep for 6 to 12 months; the exact shelf life is on each technical data sheet.
